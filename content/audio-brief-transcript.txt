Mat, the number that matters this week isn’t a Hall of Fame vote. It’s thirty-three.

Phoenix won twenty-nine games in the 2003–04 season. One year later, the Suns won sixty-two. That thirty-three-win jump remains the clearest evidence of what can happen when an organization gives an idea permission, surrounds it with complementary people, and stays with it long enough to become an identity.

Mike D’Antoni gave the idea shape. Steve Nash made the decisions. Amar’e Stoudemire created pressure at the rim. Shawn Marion made the geometry work. Shooting opened the floor. None of those pieces tells the full story alone. Together, they changed what winning basketball could look like.

Although they did not win the championship, that era gave Phoenix a durable blueprint. Not a scheme to copy. A way to build.

That is why the history feels relevant now.

Phoenix has put a new structure in place under ownership. Brian Gregory has completed a full season as general manager. Jordan Ott has completed his first season as head coach. They are no longer introducing the foundation. They are entering the next stage: reinforcing it.

There is already evidence of movement. Phoenix went from thirty-six wins to forty-five, returned to the postseason, and established clearer principles. Players described greater alignment and stability. Those are not the final results. They are the scaffolding.

In championship organizations, the system is larger than a diagram on a whiteboard. It is the connection between ownership, the front office, the coaching staff, player development, roster construction, and daily standards. Alignment has to survive pressure. Culture has to become behavior. Roles have to become clear enough that people can act decisively.

The parallel to the D’Antoni era is not that today’s Suns should recreate Seven Seconds or Less. The parallel is that Phoenix has been through this kind of building process before. An idea became a system because the organization gave it room, chose people who fit, and kept building when the work became uncomfortable.

That is the opportunity now. One year has established the base. The next phase is to add to it without losing the principles that created the progress.

The championship is still the standard. It should be. But durable results rarely appear before the structure that can sustain them. Phoenix is building that structure now.

The Hall of Fame is the recognition. The thirty-three-win jump is the evidence. And the current Suns have the opportunity to carry the lesson further—to turn permission, people, and patience into something that ends with a championship.
