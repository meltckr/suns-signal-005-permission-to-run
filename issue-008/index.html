The Revealing Detail

The camp started with breathing.

On August 22, roughly 50 children ages 11 through 14 gathered at Pecos Community Center for Oso Ighodaro’s free youth camp. The Jr. Suns/Jr. Mercury program and Ahwatukee YMCA helped put it on. Trainers and coaches from his own childhood were back in the gym. Oso called that “super special.”

Then the basketball day began with breathing and meditation.

That detail is more than a wellness note. At 24, Oso is tying together the place that formed him, the people who taught him, and a routine he now says is “definitely an important part of the game.” The calm is not separate from his competitive role. It is part of how a connector sees the floor before trying to control it.

What Availability Built

The connector role is no longer theoretical.

Official 2025–26 statistics show Oso appeared in all 82 games and started 24, averaging 22.0 minutes, 6.5 points, 5.1 rebounds, and 2.3 assists while shooting 65.3 percent. The Arizona Republic reported that only 18 NBA players appeared in every game. Oso said doing it again is a goal.

The role grew when the responsibility grew. In his 24 starts, he averaged 8.0 points, 6.3 rebounds, and 3.1 assists in 26.9 minutes. In four first-round games against Oklahoma City, all starts, he averaged 7.5 points, 7.0 rebounds, and 4.0 assists across 32.3 minutes.

Those numbers do not make him a finished player. They establish something more useful: the team has already seen his connective value survive additional minutes, starting responsibility, and playoff pressure.

The Connector’s Job

Three responsibilities are becoming one role.

01

Make continuity functional.

Oso said this is his first NBA season with this many returning players. Familiarity matters only when it improves timing, language, and trust. His description of pickup as fun is an early signal, not proof.

02

Translate intelligence into organization.

He called the additions of Koa Peat, Miles Bridges, Luke Kennard, and two-way guard Cam Spencer high-IQ, competitive players who arrive early. A connector’s value is helping those traits become shared action.

03

Use voice before volume.

Oso said his No. 1 role is to “quarterback on the floor offensively and defensively.” The word matters. Leadership here is not possession count. It is anticipation, communication, and making five players more coherent.

04

Add aggression without losing connection.

His stated next step is to be “a lot more aggressive.” The test is whether added pressure on the defense expands the connective role rather than pulling him away from it.

Where The Bet Lives

The next layer begins where the current efficiency ends.

The Arizona Republic reported that 87.5 percent of Oso’s 2025–26 shots came inside 10 feet. He made 65.3 percent from the field, a strong result built almost entirely near the basket. He attempted one three and missed it. Across two NBA seasons, he is 0-for-3 from three.

The free-throw line is the sharper evidence point. Oso made 53 of 117 last season, 45.3 percent, after making 29 of 50 as a rookie, 58 percent. He told Rankin he is working on both the free throw and the three, then kept the outcome private: “You’ll have to wait and see.”

That restraint fits the larger story. The useful question is not whether a summer workout clip looks promising. It is whether the shot becomes stable enough to change how defenses guard his handoffs, short-roll decisions, and late-clock possessions. The first signal may be the free throw before it is the three.

Why It Matters

The outside forecast and inside standard can both be useful.

ESPN’s August 24 panel projected Phoenix eighth in the West at 42–40, placing the Suns in the play-in group after a 45–37 season and seventh-place finish. That is an external forecast, not an official expectation and not a verdict on the roster.

Oso’s answer was different. He assumed the outside might expect “about the same,” while the group’s internal goal remained to compete in the playoffs and for a championship. The gap is not a reason to dismiss the forecast. It is a way to define the proof the season must produce.

Oso’s own path offers a smaller version of that problem. Outsiders can see a dependable, efficient interior connector. Inside the building, he is describing more: leadership, aggression, floor command, and expanded shooting. Availability got the role started. The next evidence will show whether the role can grow.
