Mat, Phoenix's development work has become more important before training camp even begins. There is something encouraging in that, without pretending the latest injury is good news.

On September sixth, Holden Sherman was already writing about Khaman Maluach becoming a more meaningful contributor. Five days later, the Suns announced that Mark Williams had undergone shoulder surgery. The team has not established a return timetable.

That sequence matters. The interest in the young players was there before the immediate need. The injury changes the urgency. It doesn't tell us how ready anyone is.

Maluach's four Summer League games offered a reason for interest. He averaged nineteen and a half points and nearly thirteen rebounds. That was July, against Summer League competition. It's evidence of progress in that setting, not a promise about the regular season.

For ownership, the question is broader than who fills the minutes. It's whether the preparation already taking place can become dependable contribution when circumstances change. Public reporting cannot establish the quality of the daily work inside the building. Camp will begin to make more of it visible.

The reaction outside the organization is worth separating, too. In the Suns discussion thread reviewed after the injury, some supporters were eager to see the young players get an opportunity. Others were asking for patience. That's a small, self-selected sample, not a reading of the entire fan base. But it shows how quickly excitement can turn into expectations.

Media day is Monday, September twenty-eighth. Training camp begins the following day, Tuesday, September twenty-ninth, according to Duane Rankin's reporting. Those are two different opportunities to learn. Media day gives the organization room to explain the next phase. Camp and preseason begin to show what the group can carry forward.

The league scan adds useful context. The Clippers' penalties show how a governance failure can affect both future assets and present decision-making. Toronto is still waiting for the reported Kawhi Leonard trade to be completed. And the league has announced its sixty-game NBA TV schedule, another reminder that the distribution picture continues to develop after the first schedule release.

Phoenix doesn't need to turn September into a verdict on its young players. There is room to recognize progress and keep the standard honest. The most encouraging evidence will be specific: clearer responsibilities, contributions that repeat, and a group that can keep improving when its plans change.
