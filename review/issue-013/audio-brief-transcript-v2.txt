Mat, Jordan Goodwin gave Phoenix extra chances right away in Detroit. John Voita’s game account described three offensive rebounds in his first three minutes.

Two nights later in Chicago, Mark Bowser described consecutive forced turnovers as Goodwin applied pressure and Ryan Dunn forced an eight-second violation.

That is encouraging. Players outside the starting five can improve a possession before Phoenix has to create a shot.

The week also showed the work ahead. Phoenix lost one hundred nine to one hundred seven in Detroit, and one hundred twenty-four to one hundred seventeen in Chicago. Both early advantages disappeared. In Detroit, the starters finished at halftime, and the players who followed faced much of the opponent’s starting group.

The question I would keep in view is how much of the good play carries over when the lineup changes. Watch the possessions just after a substitution and just after a mistake. Does the next group continue making the opponent work? Can the team recover its attention on the next trip?
