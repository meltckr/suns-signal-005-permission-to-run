## 01 · OWNERSHIP
### Timing Takes Two

Mat, Collin Gillespie’s description of his work with Khaman Maluach comes down to a choice that lasts only a moment: stay with the screen or leave for the rim.

In [Duane Rankin’s October 1 camp report](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/suns-coach-provides-timeline-determining-221859305.html), Gillespie described what the two are learning: “When to stick screens, when to get out early.” He talked about finding Maluach near the basket and giving him more chances to finish there.

A few lines earlier, Gillespie had described playing alongside Oso Ighodaro: “We know what to expect.”

Those two comments put the work in perspective. With one teammate, Gillespie described an understanding already built. With another, he described the details they are still learning together.

### Learning the other player

The interesting part is the adjustment on both sides of the screen.

A big man who leaves early can put pressure on the rim. Holding the screen can give the guard time to gain an advantage. The useful choice depends on the defense, but also on whether the guard and screener are ready for the same action.

An opening can be there and still disappear before both players recognize it. Their timing determines whether they can use it together.

Familiarity takes on a practical meaning here. Players can know the same play and still be learning each other’s pace inside it. Gillespie’s explanation makes the work specific: a decision they will make repeatedly, against defenders who can change the answer.

A guard can become accustomed to one teammate’s rhythm. Another teammate brings a different movement, reach and pace. Learning those cues gives the guard a chance to recognize an opening sooner. The big man is learning, too: when his teammate needs the screen and when he is ready for him to leave it.

### What a shared possession can reveal

There is value in hearing the unfinished work described this clearly. It gives us a concrete question for preseason: are they beginning to anticipate the same opening?

Monday’s opener in Detroit offers the next public look. Jordan Ott has said he wants to let preseason play out before settling the starting-center decision. Alongside that larger question, these smaller exchanges are worth following.

The tell may be an ordinary-looking play: the screen lasts long enough, the release comes as the guard is ready, and the pass arrives while the opening is there.

Timing takes two. This is a chance to watch two teammates learn it together.

## 02 · SUNS WEEKLY PULSE
### The latest from camp

#### A fuller rehearsal

Phoenix played a three-quarter scrimmage Friday. Reporting after Saturday’s practice, Duane Rankin said the team came through it healthy; Ott said many of the high-minute players logged 20–24 minutes in Friday’s scrimmage.

#### More ways to connect

Ott noted that Kennard and Ighodaro worked well together in Friday’s scrimmage. Jordan Goodwin described handling point-guard duties that let Gillespie work off the ball. These are useful early examples of teammates finding different ways to complement one another.

## 03 · AROUND THE LEAGUE
### WNBA Playoff Watch

The semifinals begin October 4: No. 4 Atlanta faces No. 8 New York, while No. 2 Golden State meets No. 3 Las Vegas. New York swept top-seeded Minnesota; Atlanta swept Washington. Golden State and Las Vegas each advanced in three games.

Both semifinal series are best-of-five. The contrast worth following is how teams carry their first-round success into a longer series, with more opportunities for opponents to adjust. For a two-franchise owner, it is a timely view of the league’s remaining contenders as the Suns begin preseason.
